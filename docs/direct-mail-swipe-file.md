# Direct mail swipe file: proven copy formulas and templates for real estate investors

Compiled by DataSift, November 2026, from DataSift's own mail data, operator tests and vendor sources. Merge fields are in [BRACKETS]. The last section shows how each one maps to DataSift Mail Design and what the builder cannot fill.

## How to use this

The best evidence here is DataSift's own data. A review of the last 980,000 mailers sent through DataSift found that three formats dominate: the real-pen letter, the snap pack check and the personal-brand postcard. The rule that came out of it: never send the same piece twice in a row.

Nobody publishes independent, per-template conversion data for investor mail. Each template below is tagged with the strongest evidence behind it, ranked this way:

1. **DataSift data:** platform volume, DataSift live trainings and DataSift customer case studies.
2. **Operator tests:** investors who say they split-tested a specific wording at volume (mostly BiggerPockets threads).
3. **Vendor claims:** mail houses selling the product. These are directional only.

The templates are rewrites of the proven structures, not anyone's copy pasted verbatim. The originals are linked in Sources so you can study them. Customer names, staff names and per-account data are left out of this copy on purpose.

## What 753,000 real DataSift sends show

Postcards carry 93% of the volume, but letters do a quarter of the campaigns. The operators who run the most campaigns rotate five or more templates. ## The formulas

Eight structures show up again and again behind proven investor mail. The winners are short, name the property and make the owner the subject.

1. **The three-line ask.** Name, property address, "I want to buy it," phone number. Nothing else.
   - Evidence: a BiggerPockets moderator says this exact shape generates a lot of response. An investor who has mailed thousands of yellow letters says split tests of that simple wording beat almost every alternative, though yellow letters as a whole now pull low response.
2. **"You," not "I."** Make the owner the subject: "Would you consider selling 123 Oak?" instead of "I want to buy your house."
   - Evidence: a common BiggerPockets critique of letters that underperform.
3. **Empathy first.** Name their situation gently, offer options and close with a low-pressure ask ("even if you're not ready"). Drop jargon like "as-is" and "no contingencies."
   - Evidence: DealDriven's 2026 copy analysis says urgency-first "fast cash" pitches now read as background noise.
4. **Say where you got their name.** One line: "I found your name in public probate records. I'm a local buyer." This defuses the "how did you get my info" reaction.
   - Evidence: one probate investor says leading with this got him a good response.
5. **Escalation, plus a new way to reply each time.** Each letter is a little more urgent than the last, and each adds a response channel (cell, email, recorded line, mail-back form, text).
   - Evidence: Jay Connor's eight-letter pre-foreclosure campaign reached a 57% cumulative response, counted per owner rather than per piece.
6. **Pattern interrupt.** Change the format on every touch: letter, then postcard, then check, then a different letter. The same piece twice in a row decays.
   - Evidence: DataSift's 980,000-mailer review, and one DataSift operator's rotation of 35,000-45,000 pieces a month.
7. **The options packet.** A printed guide to the owner's real options (keep it, sell it, negotiate with the lender), sent in a Priority Mail envelope. It costs $10-12 a piece, so save it for the leads you want most.
   - Evidence: DataSift team tests on its own highest-spread foreclosure records.
8. **The P.S. hook.** Many readers jump straight to the postscript, so put the curiosity line there: "P.S. Even if you've been told it's unsellable, call me."
   - Evidence: DataSift's Direct Mail Playbook, chapter 3.

One rule cuts the other way. Keep prices off letters and postcards so owners have to call to hear the number. The check mailer is the one exception, and it's deliberate (see the check section).

## Handwritten personal notes

These are the workhorse for niche lists. Send them as an Open Letter **Real Penned Personal Letter**: real ballpoint pen, an A6 envelope and a live Forever stamp, at $1.89 a piece in DataSift on the Business tier and up (November 2026). The working theory on why they win: they look like an RSVP or a holiday card, and the live stamp matters as much as the handwriting.

**H1. The three-liner** (the classic; the evidence is operator split tests)

```markdown
Hi [FIRST NAME],

My name is [YOUR NAME]. I'd like to buy your house at [PROPERTY ADDRESS].

Would you consider selling? Call or text me at [PHONE].

[YOUR NAME]
```

**H2. The drove-by neighbor note** (for driving-for-dollars and vacant lists; use "drove by" only when someone actually did)

```markdown
Hi [FIRST NAME],

My [wife/husband] and I live here in [CITY] and drove past your house on [STREET] last week. We'd love to buy it.

Is it something you'd ever consider selling? We can buy it as it sits, on whatever timeline works for you.

Call or text anytime: [PHONE]

Thank you,
[YOUR NAME]
```

**H3. The exhausted-record note** (one DataSift operator's play: mail only the records you couldn't reach by phone or text)

```markdown
Hi [FIRST NAME],

I've tried to reach you by phone about [PROPERTY ADDRESS], but I may have the wrong number.

If you've ever thought about selling, I'd love to talk. If not, no worries at all.

My cell is [PHONE].

[YOUR NAME]
```

**H4. The follow-up note** (a later touch; change the envelope color or switch to print handwriting so it's not the same piece twice)

```markdown
Hi [FIRST NAME],

I wrote you back in [MONTH] about your house at [PROPERTY ADDRESS]. I know life gets busy, so I wanted to try once more.

I'm still interested, and I'm flexible: as it sits, your timeline, no repairs or cleanout.

Even if you're not ready, I'm happy to answer questions. Text or call [PHONE].

[YOUR NAME]

P.S. If you've already sold or it's just not for sale, text "SOLD" and I'll stop writing.
```

The opt-out P.S. in H4 is a suggestion, not a tested line. It should cut angry calls and clean the list. Test it against a version without it, and only use it if a text to that number actually reaches someone who will take the owner off the list.

Open Letter options to rotate: cursive (the default) or print handwriting (+$0.18), and a lavender or navy envelope. Navy is hand-addressed in white gel pen.

## Probate and inherited

Probate gets the most respectful version of every formula. Say where you found their name, keep any condolence to one plain line and offer help beyond buying. Don't open probate with a check mailer or a "fast cash" postcard. A good rule for these sensitive first-to-market lists is to alternate handwritten letters with a soft postcard.

**P1. The executor letter** (Open Letter Real Penned Professional Letter: hand-addressed envelope, your typed letter inside)

```markdown
Dear [PR FIRST NAME] [PR LAST NAME],

I'm a local home buyer in [COUNTY]. I found your name in the public probate record for the estate of [DECEDENT NAME], and I'm sorry for your loss.

Settling an estate is a lot of work, and the house is often the hardest part. If the estate decides to sell [PROPERTY ADDRESS], I'd like to make an offer.

- We buy it as it sits. No repairs, no cleanout; leave whatever you don't want.
- We work around the court's timeline and with the estate's attorney.
- Every purchase closes through a licensed title company.

If selling isn't the plan, I'm glad to pass along people we trust for estate cleanouts, appraisals, or listing.

Call or text me at [PHONE], or email [EMAIL].

Sincerely,
[YOUR NAME]
[COMPANY] | [LOCAL ADDRESS]
```

**P2. The heir note** (Real Penned Personal Letter; for inherited or pre-probate records where you're writing to family, not the court-appointed representative)

```markdown
Hi [FIRST NAME],

I'm reaching out about [PROPERTY ADDRESS]. If the house has passed to you and your family and keeping it isn't the plan, I'd be glad to help.

I can buy it as it sits, belongings and all, on whatever timeline feels right.

No pressure at all. Call or text me at [PHONE] whenever you're ready.

[YOUR NAME]
```

**P3. The guide offer** (the options-packet formula adapted to probate; Open Letter Professional Letter)

```markdown
Dear [FIRST NAME],

When a family inherits a house, the same questions come up. Can we sell before probate closes? Who signs? What happens to everything inside?

I put together a free one-page guide, "Selling an Inherited House in [STATE]," that answers them in plain English. Text GUIDE to [PHONE] and I'll send it. No strings attached.

If you'd like to talk about [PROPERTY ADDRESS] itself, I'm a local buyer and happy to help.

[YOUR NAME]
```

Notes:

- **Condolences are split.** In the main BiggerPockets probate thread, some investors got angry calls over sympathy lines and others got thanks. One sincere line, or none, is the safe middle.
- **The guide has to be right for the state.** Have local counsel review it; it's general information, not legal advice.
- **Returned mail is a lead.** One DataSift operator filtered a pre-probate list for returned mail, deep-prospected those records and closed a deal within about 30 days. On death-related lists, returned mail usually means something worth digging into.
- **Write to the living decision maker.** On an obituary or pre-probate record the owner of record is often the person who died. Send P2 only once research has put the heir's name and mailing address on the record, or the letter greets the decedent.

## Pre-foreclosure, tax-delinquent, and code violations

These lists respond to options, not pitches. DataSift's own foreclosure tests found that many owners, and heirs especially, don't know a sale date is coming. Leading with the facts and three real paths builds credibility no "we buy houses" piece can.

**F1. The options letter** (pre-foreclosure first touch; Open Letter Real Penned Professional Letter)

```markdown
Hi [FIRST NAME],

Public records show [PROPERTY ADDRESS] is scheduled for a foreclosure sale on [AUCTION DATE]. You may already know this. Many owners we talk to don't, especially when a house was inherited.

You likely have more options than you think:

1. Keep the home. We can point you to a HUD-approved housing counselor and to loan modification professionals.
2. Sell before the sale date. We can buy as-is and close before [AUCTION DATE].
3. List it. We can refer you to a local agent.

The sooner you act, the more of these options stay open.

Call or text [PHONE].

[YOUR NAME]
```

**F2. The options-packet cover note** (a $10-12 Priority Mail packet with a printed foreclosure guide for your market; save it for the leads you want most)

```markdown
[FIRST NAME],

Inside is a short guide to the foreclosure process in [COUNTY]: the timeline, what happens on [AUCTION DATE], and every option you still have, including keeping the home.

We're local, and we help owners with whichever path they choose. Call or text [PHONE], or read it online at [URL].

[YOUR NAME], [COMPANY]
```

**F3. The countdown follow-up** (Jay Connor's escalation formula; add a new way to reply on every touch)

```markdown
Hi [FIRST NAME],

A quick follow-up on [PROPERTY ADDRESS]. The sale date is [AUCTION DATE], about [N] weeks away.

If calling is hard, you can text [PHONE], email [EMAIL], or leave a message 24/7 at [RECORDED LINE]. Whatever's easiest.

[YOUR NAME]
```

**T1. The tax-delinquent letter** (never write "I saw you're behind on taxes"; acknowledge the stress without naming it)

```markdown
Hi [FIRST NAME],

I buy houses in [COUNTY], and I often work with owners dealing with back taxes or other bills tied to a property.

If [PROPERTY ADDRESS] has become more burden than it's worth, I can buy it as it sits. Anything owed is paid from the sale at closing, so it's off your plate.

If you'd rather keep it, I'm also happy to point you to the county's payment options.

Call or text [PHONE].

[YOUR NAME]
```

**C1. The code-violation letter**

```markdown
Hi [FIRST NAME],

Going back and forth with the city over [PROPERTY ADDRESS] is a headache nobody wants. If you'd rather hand off the repairs and the open citations, I'll buy the house as it sits, violations and all.

Call or text me at [PHONE]. Even if you just want to hear your options, I'm happy to talk.

[YOUR NAME]
```

Compliance (have counsel review every pre-foreclosure template for each state you mail):

- **Foreclosure laws.** Several states regulate "foreclosure consultants" and "equity purchasers" and require specific written notices. [Florida](https://flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2018/0501.1377) and [New Hampshire](https://gencourt.state.nh.us/rsa/html/XLVIII/479-B/479-B-5.htm) are two examples. F1 refers owners out for the keep-the-home path instead of promising to stop the foreclosure. Keep it that way, and never offer to stop or delay a foreclosure yourself.
- **No government look-alikes.** Don't design any piece to resemble a court or government notice. The federal Deceptive Mail Prevention and Enforcement Act targets mail made to look like government documents.
- **Check auction dates before mailing.** A wrong date costs you all the credibility the letter is built on.
- **The F2 packet is a DIY or custom job.** It isn't in Open Letter's self-serve catalog. Ask Open Letter whether its managed-campaign service can produce it.

## Absentee, tired landlord, and vacant

These are mostly bulk lists, so the letters below go only to your highest-scored records. The rest of the list gets the postcard and check rotation. One DataSift operator runs it that way at about 35,000-45,000 pieces a month, each list hit 2-3 times, with the format rotating every touch.

**A1. The absentee owner letter** (name the pain of owning from a distance)

```markdown
Hi [FIRST NAME],

Owning a house in [PROPERTY CITY] while you live in [OWNER CITY] isn't easy: the calls, the repairs, the tenants, the taxes.

If you've ever thought about selling [PROPERTY ADDRESS], I'd like to buy it. You won't need to fix or clean anything, and [optional: your tenants can stay through their lease].

Call or text me at [PHONE].

[YOUR NAME]
```

**L1. The tired landlord letter** (the opening line is a classic investor hook, not a line seen tested; the seller-financing line opens a creative path)

```markdown
Hi [FIRST NAME],

Tenants, toilets, and trash calls at 2 a.m. If [PROPERTY ADDRESS] has turned into more work than it's worth, I'd like to make you an offer.

- Tenant in place? That's fine. We buy occupied rentals.
- Behind on repairs? We buy as-is.
- Like the income but not the work? Ask me about seller financing: you keep a monthly check without being the landlord.

Call or text [PHONE].

[YOUR NAME]
```

**V1. The vacant property letter** (use "looks empty" only when you've seen it or have a vacancy flag)

```markdown
Hi [FIRST NAME],

I noticed [PROPERTY ADDRESS] looks empty. A vacant house still costs taxes, insurance, and upkeep every month, and it's exposed to break-ins and weather damage.

If you'd like it off your plate, I'll buy it as it sits and handle the cleanout.

Call or text [PHONE].

[YOUR NAME]
```

Benchmark for these lists: one DataSift operator spent $66,554 on mail over 10 months and got 23 deals, about 6x return on ad spend, or $2,894 per contract. The first deal took a few months, then results compounded.

## Soft-offer check (snap pack)

The check is a pattern interrupt: it follows the personal pieces, it never opens a sequence and it's never sent twice in a row. The spec: an offer around 70-80% of the online estimate, written as an odd, specific number like $80,230, with the owner's name in the "pay to the order of" window.

Open Letter calls it the Snap Pack Check Mailer (ROS): a pressure-sealed, tear-open self-mailer. Ordered from Open Letter directly it runs $0.73-$1.01 Standard Class depending on volume, plus $0.27 for First Class, with a 200-piece minimum. Since November 20, 2026 it's also in DataSift's Mail Design as "Snap Pack Mailers": $1.15 a piece on the Business tier, First-Class postage, no minimum, and one starting template, "REISift - Snap Pack Mailer" (a letter on top, a checkmark panel in the middle and the check at the bottom).

**K1. The check face**

```markdown
PAY TO THE ORDER OF: [OWNER FULL NAME]
AMOUNT: $[OFFER, e.g. 80,230.00]
FOR: Purchase of [PROPERTY ADDRESS]
MEMO: Cash offer, good through [DATE]
NON-NEGOTIABLE. NO CASH VALUE. THIS IS NOT A CHECK.
```

**K2. The letter inside** (it defuses the lowball reaction before the owner can call to complain)

```markdown
[FIRST NAME],

This is a real offer to buy [PROPERTY ADDRESS] for $[OFFER], cash.

It's based on public records, so treat it as my starting point. If the house is in better shape than the records show, I'll come up.

- No repairs or cleanout
- No commissions or closing costs
- Close on the date you pick

To accept or talk it over, call or text [PHONE] by [DATE].

[YOUR NAME]
```

Why K2 matters: on BiggerPockets, one wholesaler mailed 1,300 check offer letters with no explanation and got five calls. None were viable, and most callers said the number was far below what they'd take. The "starting point" line tries to turn that reaction into a conversation. It's an addition and untested.

Required legal language (have counsel confirm for each state):

- **Federal:** any facsimile check must say it isn't a negotiable instrument and has no cash value.
- **New York:** "THIS IS NOT A CHECK" must be printed diagonally across the face.
- **California:** simulated checks are banned outright, and the Attorney General can seek up to $100 per violation. The only marking exception covers sample checks used to sell checks, which doesn't fit this use. Don't mail check pieces into California unless counsel signs off.

What you can send from DataSift today: the Snap Pack itself (Direct Mail > Custom Designs > New Design > Snap Pack Mailers > the REISift template). It can't merge an offer amount, so the version that works is a check with no amount: the check line reads "Call us to discuss our offer for your property," and the letter above it says why there's no number (a number from public records would only be a guess). Before you save it, swap the template's security banner wording for the not-negotiable statement, call a local number "local" rather than "toll free," and take off anything that suggests a bank or a government office (the check back reads "Federal Reserve" in the stock template). The template export does show a written-offer field in 10 templates from 7 accounts; confirm that the field fills on a proof before you build on it.

## Sequences

Each list type gets its own rhythm. Pre-foreclosure gets a touch every week until the sale date, while niche and bulk lists get one every 3-4 weeks. No piece ever repeats back to back. The check comes third on bulk lists and never goes to probate. The pre-foreclosure lane adds a new way to reply on every touch, following Jay Connor's escalation formula.

**Niche lists** (probate, inherited, no-contact records): about $6.50 per record for five touches at DataSift prices.

1. Day 0: P1 executor letter, or H3 for no-contact records (Real Penned, $1.89)
2. Day 21: PC2 branded postcard (6x9 with Street View, $0.76)
3. Day 45: P3 guide offer (Professional Letter, $1.22)
4. Day 75: PC3 stick-figure follow-up (6x9, $0.74)
5. Day 105: H4 follow-up note (Real Penned in a different envelope, $1.89)

**Pre-foreclosure** (time it to the sale date, which varies by state; in North Carolina, Jay Connor works about eight weeks from notice to sale):

1. Day 0: F1 options letter (Real Penned Professional Letter)
2. Day 7: PC4 quick question (6x9, text YES)
3. Day 14: F3 countdown, adding email and a recorded line
4. Day 21: F2 options packet, top records only ($10-12)
5. Then rotate PC3, F3 and PC4 weekly until the sale date

## Building these in DataSift Mail Design

Checked in DataSift Mail Design in November 2026.

| Swipe file field | Mail Design merge field |
| --- | --- |
| [FIRST NAME] | `{{C.FIRST_NAME}}` |
| [PROPERTY ADDRESS] | `{{C.PROPERTY_ADDRESS}}` |
| [YOUR NAME] | `{{SPF.FIRST_NAME}} {{SPF.LAST_NAME}}` |
| [PHONE] | `{{SPF.PHONE_NUMBER}}` (the campaign's tracking number) |
| Today's mailing date | `{{MISC.MAILED_DATE_MONTH_D_Y}}` |

- **Real Penned uses parentheses:** `((C.FIRST_NAME))`, `((C.PROPERTY_ADDRESS))` and so on.
- **There is no merge field** for the sale date, a filing date, the decedent's name, an offer amount or the month of an earlier letter. Write F1, F3, P1 and H4 without them ("before the sale date," "a while back") and time each piece to land when it should instead.
- **The first-name field prints the company name** when the owner has no first name (an LLC or a trust). Make a "Hi there," twin of every named piece and send company owners that one.
- **Real Penned has a fixed font**, a page fits about 12 lines, and the builder strips non-ASCII characters (curly quotes, long dashes) and braces when it saves. Write those pieces in plain text.
- **Street View postcards** print each recipient's own house photo at print time. Check it with the builder's "Download proof" before a launch.
- **Snap Pack Mailers are in DataSift's builder** (since November 20, 2026). The sheet folds in thirds: keep the letter on the top panel, leave the address window on the middle panel's left empty (it prints white), and keep every line clear of the two folds and the perforation above the check. Any check-style piece must say on its face that it isn't a negotiable instrument and has no cash value.
- **Never edit a design a live campaign uses.** The change applies to every order already placed with it. Save a new version instead.
- **Proven templates worth starting from** in DataSift's template library: the Absentee Doodle Streetview, Streetview, Family Streetview, Stickman Streetview and Handwritten postcards, and the Easy Process, Seller Comparison, Probate and Personal Picture letters.
- **Prices** in this file are DataSift's Business tier in November 2026. Check your own account's price table before you budget.
