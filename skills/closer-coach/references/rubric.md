# Closing Call Rubric (DataSift Community Edition)

Pipeline: acquisitions/closer calls. The Closer's job (per training): present the offer as a set of options, negotiate and renegotiate, overcome objections with the four frameworks, and get the signature live. Win condition: a signed purchase agreement. Grade against that job description.

Scope: this rubric grades ONLY what is audible on the call itself. CRM state, dispositions, offer logging, and any other Sift hygiene are out of scope and never scored here.

## How to Use

1. **Gate the call.** Run the Call Applicability Gate below. If the call does not qualify, log it in the Connection Log and stop; do not score it.
2. **Check auto-fails.** Scan the full transcript for any Auto-Fail Condition. If one is present, the call grade is FAIL regardless of numbers; still score the categories for coaching data.
3. **Score every applicable criterion 0-5** using the anchors. Mark a criterion N/A only if the situation for it never arose on the call (example: renegotiation criteria on a first-offer call, or the callback-booking criterion when a live signature was actually obtained). Category score = average of its non-N/A criteria. If an entire category is N/A, redistribute its weight proportionally across the remaining categories.
4. **Compute and report.** Call score = sum of (category score / 5 x category weight), scaled to 100. Assign the grade band, then fill in the Coaching Output Format with direct quotes from the transcript.

## Call Applicability Gate

This rubric applies to closer/acquisitions conversations with a decision-maker where at least one of the following is in play: deepening discovery on a handed-off lead, the money conversation, presenting or negotiating an offer, a scheduled renegotiation call, a creative-finance (terms) conversation, or locking signature/next steps. In-person appointment recordings and follow-up signing calls qualify.

**Not scored (Connection Log entry only):** voicemails, wrong numbers, non-decision-makers who cannot be converted to one on the call, calls that drop before a two-way conversation forms, and pure scheduling calls under roughly one minute with no substantive content. Connection Log entry fields: date/time, number dialed, seller name if known, outcome (voicemail / wrong number / no conversation / dropped), and the next action set in Sift. A call is never "unscorable" just because it was short: if the closer had a real conversation and lost it in the first minute, that call gets scored.

This rubric also does not grade prospecting or first-pass qualification. Per the training role definition: "you do not prospect, you do not gather correct numbers, and you do not run the first qualification pass." If the recording is actually a cold call or lead-management call, route it to the correct rubric.

### In-Person Appointment Variant

In-person appointment recordings are graded on the same criteria with the substitutions below (playbook: SCRIPT sec 6, TRAINING sec 6, ACQ sec 8). The in-person path carries heavy playbook emphasis: per the Cam Olivera data point, contract rate "jumped from about 20% on phone offers to about 50% in person."
- Expectation re-set at the door before anything else.
- Criterion 1.4: the condition pass is a physical walkthrough with photos ("photos signal seriousness... keeps the seller honest about condition").
- Criterion 2.2: the offer is presented from a pre-prepared folder.
- Criterion 4.1: signature before leaving: "We don't want to leave that property leaving them with that purchase agreement, just like we wouldn't want to send out an email with our offer either."

## Auto-Fail Conditions

An auto-fail overrides the numeric score: the call is marked FAIL, flagged for manager review, and category scores are kept for coaching only.

Sourcing note: conditions 3 and 4 are supported by the closer script and training digests. Conditions 1, 2, and 5 are company compliance policy additions and are not sourced from the closer script or training.

1. **DNC ignored.** Seller asks to be removed or says stop calling, and the closer argues, keeps pitching, or fails to acknowledge and honor it. *(Company compliance policy addition, not digest-sourced.)*
2. **Abusive conduct.** Profanity directed at the seller, shouting, sarcasm at the seller's expense, or judgmental/discriminatory remarks about the seller, their house, or their situation. *(Company compliance policy addition, not digest-sourced.)*
3. **Misrepresentation.** Lying about identity or company, inventing a fake buyer, fake deadline, fake inspection result, or false statements about the legal process. (Externalizing to "the partners" per the script is permitted; fabricating facts is not. The "underwriting department" theater called out in the benchmarks: "It's embarrassing how it's received... they read right through it and it's cringeworthy.")
4. **Legal or financial advice delivered as advice.** Due-on-sale and DTI answers must be "delivered as experience, not legal advice" and "not financial advice." Presenting them as legal/financial guarantees is an auto-fail.
5. **Disclosing another person's private information** (other sellers' terms, another party's financials, private records). *(Company compliance policy addition, not digest-sourced.)*

## Categories

Weights reflect the playbook's emphasis for this pipeline: the offer stack and the live signature are the two pillars of the Closer role, negotiation/objections next, discovery deepening supports the offer, tonality and process discipline round it out.

### Category 1: Handoff Open and Discovery Deepening (15%)

| Criterion | What Great Looks Like (playbook example) | Common Failure | Score Anchors (0, 3, 5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 Confirmation open, never re-asks the story | Opens by restating the file and confirming: "I've been reviewing the notes from your conversation with [previous team member] and I understand you're looking to sell your property at [property address] because [restate their primary motivation]... Is that all still correct?" | "So, tell me about your situation" to a warm handoff; "Making them tell it again signals that nobody was listening and resets the relationship to zero" | 0 = makes the seller repeat their story from scratch; 3 = references the file but misses motivation or timeline in the restate; 5 = full restate (motivation + timeline + address) ending in the low-effort yes |
| 1.2 Restates stakes and removes a roadblock before it is raised | Names the seller's roadblocks and stakes in their own words (tenants, foreclosure, credit) and pre-solves one: "we'll also be able to help the tenants find a place," then bridges: "But that being said, we have a few ways we can help. | 1.3 Timeline set with under-promise language | "we're going to put 21 days just as a worst-case scenario. I always want to tell you 21 days and then it gets done in seven or ten days... I just want to make sure I'm setting the right expectations." | Over-promising speed ("we can close in a week") and setting an expectation you can only miss | 0 = over-promises or never sets a closing expectation; 3 = states a timeline but without the worst-case framing; 5 = under-promise framing delivered, expectation set to beat |

| 1.5 Deal killers covered before the offer | Asks before presenting: "Would anybody be upset with you if you decided to sell this property and accept an offer without speaking with them first?" and "is there anything that you first need to figure out before you can actually sell this?" | Offer made, then blindsided by "I need to talk to my wife" or unresolved tenant logistics; "The spouse objection at close usually means this stage was skipped" | 0 = no deal-killer check and a third-party surprise lands at close; 3 = checks decision-makers OR logistics, not both; 5 = decision-makers, logistics, and timeline all confirmed before the number |

### Category 2: Money Conversation and Offer Presentation (25%)

| Criterion | What Great Looks Like (playbook example) | Common Failure | Score Anchors (0, 3, 5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 Extracts the seller's number and the pocket number | Pulls their figure first: "What is a ballpark figure that you would like to sell the property for?" and structures to the net: "A seller who says '200K' with a 150K mortgage may really just want about 50K in hand. You structure to that pocket number" | Blurting a number first with no information; "NEVER give your price first... sometimes you'll out negotiate yourself" | 0 = closer blurts a number with no condition or motivation information and cedes the price frame; 3 = gets the headline ask but never probes what the seller actually nets or needs in hand; 5 = seller's number extracted AND pocket number identified, offer structured to it |
| 2.2 Presents the multi-option descending offer | All three options, fastest to highest price: "The Cash Offer: Our all-cash offer is [$X]... The Novation Offer: ...we can offer you [$Y]... in about 60 to 90 days. The Listing Option: ...You could likely get around [$Z]. Which of these options sounds best for your situation?" In person: the offer is presented from a pre-prepared folder, after re-setting expectations at the door | One take-it-or-leave-it number; the role definition explicitly forbids presenting a single number | 0 = single number, no options; 3 = two options or all three but out of order/missing the "which one" choice question; 5 = full three-option stack in descending-convenience order, ends with the seller choosing |
| 2.3 Uses contrast as the anchor | "The descending order is also the anchor: the higher listing number makes the cash number feel like the convenient choice"; higher options introduced "in the same breath as the cash number" | Presenting cash alone and defending it; or apologizing for the cash number | 0 = cash number presented in isolation and defended; 3 = options mentioned but not used as contrast (cash not framed as the convenient choice); 5 = contrast framing lands, "converts the call from a yes/no on one number into a which-one decision" |
| 2.4 Works inside the seller's numbers before correcting | Accepts their figures and runs the math out loud: "These are the sellers numbers. He gave me the 680 ARV... I would never want to immediately disagree. I would always want to use it and then combat it later on." | "Zillow is wrong, your house is not worth that" lectures; forum consensus: repair lectures mean nothing to a seller | 0 = opens by attacking the seller's valuation; 3 = accepts their numbers but never runs the math out loud or ties it back; 5 = math run in the seller's own terms first, comps only introduced after the math is agreed |
| 2.5 Pre-negotiates flex and reframes to net proceeds | Tests wiggle room before contract: "if buyers are like hey we've got to come down on price to 490, 480, is that worth it to you guys...?" and moves to what they net: "A lower number they actually walk away with, on a date they can count on, often beats a higher one" | Arguing the headline number head-on; discovering at renegotiation that there was never any flex | 0 = headline-price argument only; 3 = net framing OR flex test, not both; 5 = both: flexibility tested and the conversation moved to certain net proceeds |
| 2.6 Transparency when asked about intentions | Tells the truth about the model and even the fee: "I'd probably want to wholesale this one... probably want to make somewhere around $10 to $15,000 assignment fee and that's it." "Build the credibility with the truth" | Dodging the question, fake-department theater, vague "my partners will decide" | 0 = deceptive or evasive answer to a direct intentions question; 3 = truthful but hedged/uncomfortable, credibility not built; 5 = full transparency delivered confidently (score N/A if the seller never asks) |

Contested-edge note on 2.1: per the benchmarks digest, "Rick Ginn says do not be religious about who says a number first if a deal can come together; grade for control of the price frame, not the ritual." A closer who deliberately names a number after full condition and motivation discovery while keeping frame control is not a 0; score frame control, not turn order.

### Category 3: Objection Handling and Negotiation (20%)

| Criterion | What Great Looks Like (playbook example) | Common Failure | Score Anchors (0, 3, 5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 Treats objections as information, diagnoses the fear | Maps every stall to one of the four fears: "almost all of them resolve to one of four seller fears: Price, Time, Third Party, or Trust," and hesitation on a contract section "reveals exactly where the real objection lives" | Treating objections as walls: arguing, repeating the pitch louder, or folding instantly | 0 = argues with or ignores objections; 3 = responds to objections but never isolates which fear is underneath; 5 = names/diagnoses the fear and answers it specifically |
| 3.2 Deploys the correct framework | Name the No: "a lot of times when people say, let me think about it, it's a polite way of saying no." Box the Objection: "Is it the idea of working with me?... Is it the process?... Is it the price? Are you considering other options?" Acknowledge and Redirect (program name "Deflect & Redirect"): "We received your information from our third party marketing team... Have you thought about selling now or in the near future?" (fits early-call and how-did-you-get-my-info moments, which do still occur on handoff calls) | Accepting "I'll think about it" as a real state and promising a vague callback with no diagnosis | 0 = smokescreen accepted at face value, call ends undiagnosed; 3 = one framework attempted but incomplete (labels the no, never boxes); 5 = framework matched to the moment and run fully, real objection surfaced |
| 3.3 Best-Price Ask, once only | "is there a price you have in mind that if I can get approved for you, you would award us the opportunity to assist you and not go with any other companies?" Rule: ask once, "shoot your shot," never re-ask | Re-asking, bidding against himself, or discounting before being asked ("you stop discounting before you are asked") | 0 = negotiates against himself (unprompted discount or re-asked best price); 3 = asks the best-price question but muddies it with extra concessions; 5 = clean single ask, then silence, no self-negotiation anywhere on the call (N/A if seller was never shopping) |
| 3.4 Third-party objections converted to momentum | Spouse: "why don't I go ahead and write you up a contract, send it over so you and your wife can review it." Attorney: "I'd like to send the written offer to them directly... What is their direct contact information?... When can we commit to getting on a call to review what you and your attorney discussed?" | Objection ends the call: "okay, talk to your wife and call me back," no paper moving, no committed review call | 0 = third-party objection ends the call with nothing booked; 3 = handles it verbally but gets neither the contract moving nor a committed review time; 5 = paper moving AND a specific committed next call, plus the feel-check ("I kind of get the feeling...") (N/A if no third-party objection arose) |
| 3.5 Terms pivot used as a tool, not a default | When price beats max cash and motivation is real: "if we could get closer to that number, would you be open to a different way of structuring the sale? I give you the price you want, you give me the terms I need," plus the diagnostic: "Which is more important to you right now: getting the absolute highest price... or getting cash in your hand as quickly as possible?" | One-tool caller: "cash offer rejected, call over. No pivot question ever asked." Or pivoting to terms on every call regardless of cues ("The pivot is a tool, not a default") | 0 = deal dies on cash with no pivot attempted despite live motivation; 3 = pivot attempted but without the price-vs-terms diagnostic or against the gating cues; 5 = pivot triggered by the correct cues, hinge line and diagnostic both used (N/A if cash worked or motivation was absent) |
| 3.6 Renegotiation run by the book | Separate scheduled call, empathy open ("I've been dreading this call all day long... I just feel absolutely horrible"), externalized ("The partners let me know that the inspection report came back"), named repairs, acknowledged floor ("I know you're wanting [original price]... [Pause, listen intently]"), reassurance, new number, hand-back: "What are your thoughts on that?" | Casual mid-call price drop; dropping the number with no named repairs; steamrolling past the seller's stated floor | 0 = price dropped casually mid-call or with no grounding in named issues; 3 = structure partially followed (number grounded but empathy/pause/hand-back missing); 5 = full sequence: empathy, externalize, named issues, acknowledge floor, pause, reassure, new number, hand back agency (N/A if not a renegotiation call) |

## Grade Bands

| Band | Score | Coaching Directive |
|---|---|---|
| Elite | 90-100 | Pull quotes for the training library and have this closer run the next film-review session. |
| Strong | 75-89 | One targeted fix: drill the single lowest-scoring criterion until it is automatic, everything else is working. |
| Developing | 60-74 | Rebuild the two weakest categories with script-aloud reps and role-play before the next live offer call. |
| Needs Work | 40-59 | Back to structure: manager listens live or same-day on every offer call this week, script open on screen. |
| Retrain | Below 40 | Off live offers; full closer-script retraining and graded role-plays before dialing again. |

## Tonality Evaluation Guide

Grade tonality only from what is observable in the transcript (plus delivery notes if the transcription includes them). Do not score "sounded bad" without a countable behavior.

**Phrasing markers (count them):**
- Positive: seller's name used naturally; mirrors and labels ("So, if I understand correctly...", "It sounds like..."); casual softeners ("no worries," "just kind of curious"); same-team framing; benefit-to-them framing. At least one mirror or label per call is the floor.
- Negative: filler density ("um, uh, like, you know" as a rate per minute, rising density undermines authority); hedging pile-ups; fake urgency or fake-department phrasing; any negative remark about the house or the situation.

**Turn structure (infer from line lengths and speaker turns):**
- Talk-listen balance: on discovery and objection segments the seller should own the longest turns; a closer monologue is acceptable only during the three-option presentation and the six-section walkthrough. If the closer's lines dwarf the seller's everywhere, flag it.
- Question discipline: open-ended questions followed by a seller answer of substance. A question immediately followed by more closer text means he answered his own question. Count these.
- Interruptions: talk-overs marked in the transcript (cutoffs, double-speech markers). Zero is the target at decision moments; 2+ drags 5.2 down.
- Silence honored: after the Best-Price Ask, after "you wouldn't take a penny less," and after each contract section, the next voice must be the seller's.

**Empathy beats:** on any loss or distress disclosure, look for a brief sincere acknowledgment within the next closer turn ("I'm sorry to hear about your mom's passing"), then forward motion. Absence of the beat, or three consecutive turns of wallowing, both fail.

**Audio cues (only if delivery notes are present):** pace notes (rushed reading vs conversational), volume matching (great closers get quieter when the seller gets louder), audible script reading, dramatic pauses on the apology/renegotiation lines ("Delivered with specific tonality and dramatic pauses"). If no delivery notes exist, score tonality from the text proxies above only.

## Perfect Call Definition

A 100 call opens with the closer restating the seller's motivation, timeline, and address from the notes and earning a "yes, that's all still correct" without the seller repeating a word of their story. Deal killers, roadblocks, and big-ticket condition are covered conversationally before any number, the timeline is set at 21 days worst-case, and the closer pulls the seller's ballpark and their true pocket number before pricing anything. The offer arrives as the full descending three-option stack (cash, novation, listing) ending in "Which of these options sounds best for your situation?", the math is run inside the seller's own numbers, and every stall is diagnosed to Price, Time, Third Party, or Trust and answered with the matching framework, with the Best-Price Ask fired exactly once if the seller is shopping. The moment the seller picks, the agreement is on their screen and signed live after a six-section walkthrough with real pauses, or, if signing is physically impossible, a firm either-or callback is booked and confirmed before the call ends. Throughout, the closer sounds like a calm human ally who is visibly willing to lose the deal: silence after every ask, a sincere beat on every hard disclosure, transparency when asked intentions, and not one syllable of desperation, self-negotiation, or robotic reading.
