# Cold Calling Call Rubric (DataSift Community Edition)

Governing frame: on a first-touch cold call we are correct-number-gatherers, not deal-closers. The Cold Caller's four jobs in priority order: (1) confirm the decision-maker, (2) gather correct contact info, (3) capture early motivation (target: 2 of the 4 Pillars), (4) book the next call with expectations set. A call that closes nothing but confirms the decision-maker, captures honest motivation, and books a defined next call is a WIN. Grade against that job description, not against "did they close."

Scope: this rubric grades ONLY what is audible on the call itself. CRM state, dispositions, record notes, and any other Sift hygiene are out of scope and never scored here.

## How to Use (grading a transcript plus the call's Sift record and notes, 4 steps max)

1. **Gate the call.** Run the Applicability Gate below. If the call does not qualify, log it in the Connection Log and stop. No score.
2. **Scan for auto-fails.** Read the full transcript once for any Auto-Fail Condition. If one is present, the call grade is FAIL (still score categories for coaching, but the headline is Fail and it goes to manager review).
3. **Score every criterion 0 to 5** using the anchors. Mark a criterion N/A only if the seller made it unreachable (example: seller hung up before a close was possible) or the transcript does not preserve the signal the criterion measures (fillers stripped, no pause or crosstalk annotations). Category score = average of its non-N/A criteria. If an entire category is N/A, redistribute its weight proportionally across the scored categories.
4. **Compute and report.** Call score = sum of (category score / 5 x category weight), scaled to 100. Assign the Grade Band and fill in the Coaching Output Format with direct quotes.

## Call Applicability Gate

This rubric applies to: outbound first-touch dials to property owners (or estate decision-makers) from courthouse or niche data, where a live human answered and at least one substantive exchange about the property occurred.

Do NOT score, log a **Connection Log entry** instead (caller, number, timestamp, outcome, next disposition):
- Voicemails (note whether a voicemail was left and its quality as a comment, not a score)
- Wrong numbers with no property conversation (log as wrong number, flag record for re-skip)
- Immediate hangups or dead air under roughly 15 seconds with no exchange
- Calls where no decision-relevant human was ever reached (child, employee, answering service that took a message)

**Short calls are their own report type, never a /100 score.** A short call means the seller DECLINED: a "no," "not interested," "not for sale," opt-out, or hangup inside roughly the first 30 seconds, with no substantive property conversation beyond the decline. A brief call that ends with a callback arranged, a deferral ("call me next week"), or any engagement beyond the decline is a FULL call: grade it on the full template and mark unreachable categories N/A. When in doubt, grade it as a full call. For true short calls do NOT compute a weighted total or a grade band: a total built from two reachable criteria is not comparable with a full-call score and must not sit in the same ranking column. Write a SHORT CALL REPORT instead (template in Coaching Output Format): score the reachable Opener criteria (0-5 each) and the "Converting the no" criterion (0-5), record whether any conversion attempt was made, then give one fix and one drill. Scorecards aggregate short calls separately: count, average opener score, average conversion score, and percent of short calls with a conversion attempt. Short calls are never averaged into full-call totals.

## Auto-Fail Conditions

An auto-fail overrides the numeric score. Headline grade = FAIL, manager review required. Category scores are still recorded for coaching. Keep this list short and objective; nothing subjective ("bad tone") belongs here.

1. **Opt-out ignored or argued.** Any "take me off your list" or "stop calling" that is not honored immediately and without pushback.
2. **Abusive conduct.** Profanity directed at the seller, shouting, sarcasm at the seller's expense, or any discriminatory remark.
3. **Misrepresentation or lying.** Fake identity or company, fake buyer, fake deadline or manufactured scarcity, fake departments or partners ("let me talk to my underwriting department" theater), or false statements about the legal process.
4. **Harassment continuation.** Continuing to pitch after a clear, repeated no.
5. **Compliance breach evident on the call.** Call clearly placed outside 8 AM to 9 PM recipient local time, or an AI voice used on the outbound dial (the AI-voice check is made from the recording or dial metadata supplied with the transcript, not from transcript text).
6. **Privacy violation.** Disclosing another person's private information to the seller.

## Categories

Weights reflect what the playbook emphasizes for this pipeline: pillar capture and the booked next call are the win conditions, the opener earns the right to both, and objection handling plus tonality are how the call survives long enough to win. All five categories are pure call quality.

### Category 1: Opener and Decision-Maker Confirmation (15%)

| Criterion | What Great Looks Like | Common Failure | Score Anchors (0, 3, 5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decision-maker confirmed first | "Hey, is this [name]?" before investing in the call. On estate lists: "Are you the person handling the estate for [name]?" | Delivers the pitch to a tenant, spouse, or non-owner; Zach Ginn's rule ignored: "if they're not the owner, get off the phone" | 0 = never confirms who answered, proceeds anyway; 3 = confirms the name but not ownership or estate authority when the list required it; 5 = person and decision authority confirmed in the first exchange |
| Soft "plans" frame with the specific property | "I was just calling about the property on [street], wanted to see if you had any plans for it?" Names the seller and the exact street, one simple question, no company monologue | The dead blunt open "are you interested in selling your house?" with no context, or a generic "we buy houses for cash in your area" pitch with no name or address | 0 = generic pitch, no name or street, hard sales frame; 3 = names the property but leads with "interested in selling" or a value-prop speech; 5 = name plus specific street plus the plans question (or a sanctioned variant), then stops talking |
| Awkwardness named (pattern interrupt) | "I know this is a little random" or "I know this might seem a little out of the blue" delivered before the ask | Launching straight into a 40-second value proposition without a breath or a yes; the seller's guard stays up and the hangup follows | 0 = no acknowledgment, telemarketer launch; 3 = softener present but rushed past, no pause; 5 = clean acknowledgment, one curious question, then silence |
| Escalation discipline | Permission move ONLY on hesitation: "If it's not a good time, I can certainly call back later, or if you're not interested at all, that's perfectly fine too." Fallback ONLY after the soft approach gets nothing: "Just out of curiosity, would you have any interest in selling now or in the near future?" | Dumping the permission and fallback lines unprompted in the first breath, or jumping straight to pressure when the seller was still engaged | 0 = no ladder, either pure pressure or instant surrender; 3 = right lines, wrong trigger (used without hesitation, or skipped when needed); 5 = each rung used only when its trigger appeared |

### Category 2: Four Pillars Discovery (30%)

| Criterion | What Great Looks Like | Common Failure | Score Anchors (0, 3, 5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motivation asked soft, then mined | "So about the property, I am curious, if you did decide to sell whether to us or anyone else, why would you?" then silence. Peels layers: "Tell me more about that..." | Yes/no motivation questions ("Are you motivated to sell?"), accepting "financial reasons" or "just exploring options" at face value, talking past the reveal | 0 = never asked; 3 = asked but closed-ended, or the first surface answer accepted with zero follow-up; 5 = open why question, silence honored, at least one layer peeled and the answer captured in the seller's own words |
| Timeline pinned | "What timeframe do you feel is comfortable for you and your family?" Timeline treated as the motivation lie detector | Never asked, or asked as a checkbox with the answer ignored (a "6 months out" seller still treated as hot, or urgent seller not flagged) | 0 = not asked on an engaged seller; 3 = asked but answer not confirmed or acknowledged; 5 = asked with the comfortable-for-you framing, answer acknowledged and used to shape the rest of the call |
| Condition earned and non-judgmental | "This sounds like a property we might be interested in buying. It really just depends on the condition of the property. Can you tell me about it, like the age of the roof, age of the furnace, things like that?" Big-ticket items, seller allowed to vent, zero negativity about the house | Appraisal-style checklist fired early ("How many bedrooms? How many baths?"), interrogation feel, or any negative comment about the property | 0 = not asked, or the caller criticized the house; 3 = asked but before earning it, or read as a rapid checklist; 5 = feedback line earns the right, concrete big-ticket examples given, open flow |
| Price last, ballpark, max two asks | "What is a ballpark figure that you would like to sell the property for?" If held back, ONE reframe: "just to give us a starting point, what is a ballpark figure..." then move on | Price raised before the other pillars, the caller's own number offered first, or the ballpark pushed a third time. "A price stated early becomes an anchor the seller defends for the rest of the call" | 0 = caller quoted a price or opened with price talk; 3 = ballpark asked but too early in the call, or pushed past the second attempt; 5 = asked last, ballpark framing, stopped cleanly at two attempts |
| Order kept, flow flexible | M, T, C, P order followed, but whichever pillar the seller volunteers gets crossed off and the caller keeps flowing (never "wait, I didn't ask condition yet"). Target: at least 2 pillars captured | Script-order rigidity that blocks volunteered answers, or an engaged seller hung up with 0 to 1 pillars captured | 0 = fewer than 2 pillars captured on a willing seller; 3 = pillars covered but rigid sequencing fought the seller's flow; 5 = all reachable pillars covered in natural order, 2+ captured in the seller's own words |

### Category 3: Objection Handling and List Sensitivity (20%)

| Criterion | What Great Looks Like | Common Failure | Score Anchors (0, 3, 5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| "How did you get my info" owned | "It looks like our marketing team may have had the wrong information. That's our fault. I'm sorry, we really goofed this one up." Slight pause, genuinely apologetic. "You take the blame, there is nothing to fight" | Getting defensive or technical about data sources, or dodging the question entirely | 0 = defensive, technical, or evasive; 3 = deflected politely but no ownership taken; 5 = blame taken with the apologetic line, then conversation resumed naturally |
| List-type do-not-say discipline | Distress never named. On any distress list: "Hey, I was just calling about 123 Main Street, to see if you had any plans for it." "Death" softened to "estate" or "inherited property" | Any prohibited line: "I see you're in foreclosure," "I'm sorry for your loss" as an opener, "I know you're behind on taxes," "Your house is empty," "Are you tired of being a landlord?" | 0 = any do-not-say line used; 3 = no direct violation but the framing brushed the distress (seller had to name it defensively); 5 = clean curiosity framing throughout, seller never made to feel surveilled |
| Converting the no | "Okay, no worries. Now, is that no now or just no never?" plus a fish before the exit, either the referral ask: "Do you know someone who might be interested in a cash offer for their property?" or the equally valid other-properties ask: "Do you possibly have any other properties that you may consider selling now or in the near future?" ("I've gotten so many deals done from asking that last question"), then the courteous exit "That's ok. Hope you have a great day." | "Okay, thanks, bye" on the first no, arguing with the no, or begging. A fast no beats a long maybe, but a no still gets one conversion attempt and a referral ask | 0 = argued with the no or hung up with no attempt; 3 = one attempt made but no referral ask or abrupt exit; 5 = no-now vs no-never separated, referral asked, polite close |
| Delay and busy handled small | "I completely understand you're busy. To respect your time, this next call is designed to be quick, only 5 to 10 minutes... Would tomorrow at 10 AM or 2 PM work better?" | Arguing with "I'm busy," or surrendering to "call me sometime" with no specific time captured | 0 = argued or abandoned with nothing booked; 3 = rescheduled but vague (no specific time); 5 = time respected, next step made small, either-or specific time offered |
| Address earned, not demanded | Engagement built first, then: "Just to make sure I have the correct property in Sift, could you confirm the full address for me?" | Demanding the address up front before earning it: "You earn the address by being someone worth giving it to, not by demanding it up front" | 0 = demanded up front or never attempted when missing; 3 = asked at an awkward moment before real engagement; 5 = asked after engagement with the Sift confirmation framing, address captured |

### Category 4: Tonality and Call Control (15%)

All criteria here are transcript-observable. See the Tonality Evaluation Guide for measurement rules.

| Criterion | What Great Looks Like | Common Failure | Score Anchors (0, 3, 5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Silence honored after questions | "After each question, stop talking and let them answer." In the transcript: the seller is the next speaker after every pillar question, and their answers run longer than the caller's questions | Filling every pause, answering own questions, talking over the seller mid-reveal. "Most reps lose information by talking over the pause" | 0 = caller routinely answers own questions or interrupts (2+ talk-overs); 3 = mostly honored with one or two rescues of silence; 5 = every question followed by seller speech, zero interruptions |
| Human, not script-reading | Internalized framework with casual softeners: "just kind of curious," "okay, cool," "no worries." Sounds like a calm local human. "Just be you" | Robotic script cadence, hostage-note delivery, bracketed placeholders read aloud ("the property on street name"), identical phrasing repeated verbatim | 0 = placeholder read aloud or unmistakable line-recital throughout; 3 = script followed correctly but stiff, few natural connectives; 5 = all required beats hit in natural conversational language |
| Mirroring and active listening | Reflects content back: "So, if I understand correctly, the tenants haven't been paying rent consistently." Mirrors last words as a question: "When you say [their words], what do you mean by that?" | Robotic acknowledgments ("gotcha... gotcha... gotcha") with nothing reflected, moving to the next scripted question regardless of the answer | 0 = zero reflection, answers ignored; 3 = acknowledgments present but content rarely reflected; 5 = at least one clean mirror or summary label, follow-ups built from the seller's actual words |
| Calm and scarce, never desperate | "We stay calm and slightly scarce" and "play hard to get, not desperate." When the seller discloses a loss or pain, one brief sincere beat, then forward: "How long has that been going on? Man, that sounds really frustrating to have to deal with." | Begging, over-explaining, chasing a no, commission breath. Or zero empathy beat when the seller discloses a loss, or wallowing in the condolence and never advancing | 0 = desperate chasing or a loss disclosure completely ignored; 3 = composed but missed one empathy beat or over-explained once; 5 = calm throughout, brief empathy where earned, always advancing |
| Filler-word discipline | "It's better just to not say something than to say um." Clean, unhurried sentences | Heavy "um, uh, like, you know" density throughout; rushed telemarketer cadence visible in run-on transcript lines | 0 = filler-saturated, multiple per caller turn; 3 = noticeable fillers but message stays clear; 5 = minimal fillers, short clean turns |

### Category 5: Close and Next Step (20%)

| Criterion | What Great Looks Like | Common Failure | Score Anchors (0, 3, 5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time and Agenda set | "It should only take 5 to 10 minutes" plus "we review what the property qualifies for" (an agenda without promising a number) | Vague endings: "we'll be in touch," "I'll follow up with you sometime." "Skipping any of the four is the most common cause of a ghost" | 0 = neither time nor agenda stated; 3 = one of the two stated; 5 = both stated plainly, no number promised |
| Result and Permission given | "At the end of that call, all we need is a simple yes or no" plus "If it's not for you, that is perfectly fine" (the single biggest anti-ghosting move) | Pressure close with no exit offered, or no defined decision for the next call, leaving the seller unsure what they agreed to | 0 = neither present; 3 = one of the two present; 5 = both present, verbatim or natural equivalent |
| Deal killers surfaced before the close | "Would anybody be upset with you if you decided to sell this property and accept an offer without speaking with them first?" plus "Mr. Seller, is there anything that you first need to figure out before you can actually sell this?" Both the third-party blocker (spouse, sibling, tenant, partner) and the logistics blocker checked before booking. "Do not forget to cover deal killers, that is one of the most important parts" | Booking a partner call with a hidden spouse, co-owner, tenant, or unresolved logistics still lurking behind the yes | 0 = never asked on an engaged seller who reached booking; 3 = one of the two blockers checked, the other skipped; 5 = both third-party and logistics blockers checked before the close |
| Specific either-or booking with partner intro | "I'd like to get my partner on the line to ask you about the condition... Are you available tomorrow at 10 AM or 2 PM?" The partner intro protects the gatherer frame | Open-ended "when works for you?" that lands nowhere, no partner introduced, or no booking attempted on an interested seller | 0 = no booking attempt on an interested seller; 3 = booking attempted but open-ended or no partner intro; 5 = either-or specific times plus the partner introduced by role |
| Role discipline: no closing on call one | Gatherer frame held: "My partner handles the final numbers and the deeper qualification." No offers, no numbers, no close attempts | "The moment you try to close on call one, you break the frame and lose the lead." Quoting a price, hinting at offers, or attempting verbal agreement | 0 = made an offer or quoted a number; 3 = flirted with numbers or over-promised outcomes; 5 = frame held completely, all money talk deferred to the partner call |

**Scoring math:** each criterion 0 to 5. Category score = average of its non-N/A criteria. Call score = sum over categories of (category score / 5 x weight), giving 0 to 100.

## Grade Bands

- **90-100 Elite:** Save the recording as a training tape and have this caller run the next team roleplay.
- **75-89 Strong:** Pick the single lowest-scoring category and assign one targeted drill this week.
- **60-74 Developing:** Rebuild the two weakest categories with scripted roleplay before the next dial session.
- **40-59 Needs Work:** Back to the script daily: side-by-side call review with a lead and roleplay every morning until two consecutive calls score 60+.
- **Below 40 Retrain:** Pull from live dials; full script recertification, shadowing, and a passed roleplay assessment before returning to the phone.

## Tonality Evaluation Guide

Score tonality ONLY from what the transcript shows. Never guess at voice qualities the text cannot prove.

**Countable proxies (use these):**
- **Phrasing markers, positive:** "I know this is a little random" or "out of the blue" (pattern interrupt present), "just out of curiosity," "no worries," "okay, cool," the seller's name used naturally more than once, "whether to us or anyone else," "comfortable for you and your family," "ballpark figure."
- **Phrasing markers, negative:** a context-free "are you interested in selling your house?" hard open (the softened SIMPLE variant with the out-of-the-blue acknowledgment is sanctioned), any do-not-say line, fake urgency language ("limited spots," "act now"), fake departments.
- **Talk-listen balance (inferred from line lengths):** the caller may carry the uninvited opening, but across discovery the seller should own the longest turns, per Rick Ginn's 80/20 rule: "80% of the time you're using these two things God gave you, 20% of the time you're asking very directed guided questions." Flag any call where the caller's turns dominate discovery.
- **Silence honored:** after each pillar question, the next transcript line should be the seller. A caller line immediately following their own question (self-answering) is a silence violation. Count them.
- **Interruptions:** count talk-overs if the transcript marks them (crosstalk, cut-off markers, "[interrupts]"). Zero is ideal, 2+ drags the tonality score.
- **Question quality:** weight open-ended questions over yes/no questions; count open vs closed. Apply Cam Olivera's interrogation test: "You need to get the information without making it feel like it's an interrogation," alongside Rick Ginn's 80/20 rule quoted above. A call that reads as a barrage of directed questions with little listening fails the test.
- **Filler rate:** count "um, uh, like, you know" per caller turn. Score as a rate, not an absolute, and only if the transcript preserves fillers.
- **Mirroring and labels:** count instances of repeating the seller's last 1 to 3 key words as a question, "Tell me more about that," "It sounds like...," and summary reflections ("So, if I understand correctly..."). At least one on any engaged call.
- **Empathy beats:** when the seller discloses a loss, distress, or frustration, look for one brief sincere acknowledgment that then advances ("Man, that sounds really frustrating to have to deal with."). Distinguish this from the prohibited proactive condolence opener on obituary lists: responding to their disclosure is required, naming their distress from the list is banned.
- **Audio cues if present:** honor delivery notes embedded in the transcript ([PAUSE], [laughs], tone annotations from the transcription pass). A caller who pauses after the marketing-team apology line, or who gets the seller laughing, earns credit here.

## Perfect Call Definition

A 100 call opens with the seller's name and the exact street inside the first two sentences, confirms the right decision-maker before anything else, names its own randomness, and asks the soft "any plans for it?" question, then goes quiet. The seller talks more than the caller from that point on. Motivation, timeline, condition, and price surface in that order (or get crossed off as the seller volunteers them), each captured in the seller's own words, with the ballpark ask coming last, framed low-stakes, and never pushed past two attempts. Any snag ("how did you get my number?") is met with owned blame and zero defensiveness, the distress behind the list is never named, and the address is earned late with the Sift confirmation line. The caller never quotes a number, never tries to close, and ends by booking a specific either-or time with the partner introduced, time boxed at 5 to 10 minutes, the agenda stated without promising a number, the result defined as a simple yes or no, and explicit permission to say no. Before hangup the caller has confirmed, on the call itself, the seller's name, best number, and the property address. The seller hangs up feeling curious and respected, not sold.
