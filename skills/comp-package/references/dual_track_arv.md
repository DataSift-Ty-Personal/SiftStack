# Dual-Track ARV: the bedroom-band rule

## The rule

A subject whose bedroom count is below the comp set lives in a materially lower value band, and the gap is bigger than a standard per-bedroom adjustment ($5K/bedroom) implies. The buyer pool and the appraisal band both shift at 3 bedrooms.

Real example (Knoxville 37914 Burlington pocket, July 2026): renovated 2-beds capped at $215-280K while similar-size renovated 3/2s ran $285-385K in the same pocket. A brand-new 688 sqft 3/2 sold for $265K, beating the entire 2-bed band at a third of the size. Bedroom count beat square footage.

## The method

Bucket renovated/retail comps (sold >= 90% of Zestimate), then:

**Base track (underwrite here): same-bed comps only.**
- ARV = median $/sf of same-bed renovated comps x subject sqft
- CLAMP to the same-bed band's MEDIAN sold price: an oversized subject still sells to the same buyer pool, so extra sqft cannot push it past the middle of the band
- If fewer than 3 same-bed comps: widen to +/-1 bed and discount the result 10%, and flag it
- Adjust within the band for micro-position (the west end of a pocket can trade 10-20% under the east end)

**Upside track (labeled, never assumed): higher-bed comps.**
- Same math over comps with more bedrooms, sized within 60-140% of subject
- Cap at the 75th percentile of that band
- Credit this track ONLY after a walkthrough verifies the layout actually converts: plumbing runs, window egress, framing, ceiling heights. Sight-unseen, it does not exist
- The upside is the BUYER's room, not your underwrite. Show it so the buyer conversation lands, but contract off the base track

## Future value

The same rule applies forward: a 2-bed appreciates on the 2-bed curve. Do not project higher-bed appreciation into hold models or dispo pitches unless the reconfig actually happens.

## Wholesale math

- Flipper MAO = 70% x BASE ARV - rehab (75% in hot pockets)
- Contract target = full-gut MAO minus your assignment fee
- For a stalled/partial renovation, underwrite full gut until walked: prior work often gets redone, and unpermitted work is a real risk on auction properties
