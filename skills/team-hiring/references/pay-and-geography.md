# Where to hire each role, and what to pay

Pick the country before the salary. Pay is a function of where the role sits,
and matching the role to the right labor market is one of the largest levers
available in this business.

A data manager costs roughly $3,000 a month in the United States. The same work,
done to the same standard, runs a fraction of that in the Philippines. That gap
is not a quality tradeoff when the role is matched correctly. It is a quality
tradeoff when it is not, which is why the mapping below matters more than the
numbers.

## The mapping, and the reasoning behind it

Match the country to what the role actually demands. Roles that spend the day
talking to American sellers need a specific spoken-English and accent profile.
Roles that spend the day in a spreadsheet do not, so paying for that profile is
wasted money.

| Role | Works well from | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Data manager | Philippines, India | Process and spreadsheet work. No live seller contact, so accent is not a factor. Strong documentation culture and reliable overlap hours. |
| Prospector (cold calling) | Mexico, Egypt, Colombia | Live conversations with American sellers. Neutral accents and strong conversational English. Mexico currently reads slightly stronger than Egypt, and Egypt has become common in this market. |
| Lead manager | Mexico, Colombia | Longer relationship-driven conversations. Same accent requirement as prospecting, plus more judgment. |
| Acquisitions manager | Mexico, or domestic | Negotiation and closing. The talent is genuinely there remotely, and it is dramatically cheaper than the domestic equivalent. |
| Dispo | Domestic, or Mexico | Buyer relationships, often with repeat local investors who expect to reach a person. |

India is a strong fit for data work and a weak fit for cold calling in this
market, and that is about how American sellers react on the phone rather than
about capability.

## Pay bands

Monthly base in USD. Treat these as the middle of the market rather than a
ceiling, and pay toward the top of the band you can support.

| Role | Base per month | Commission |
|---|---|---|
| Data manager | $600 to $900 | None, or a small monthly bonus tied to consistency |
| Prospector | $700 to $1,200 | Per qualified lead passed, or a small per-contract bonus |
| Lead manager | $1,500 | 1% to 3% of closing |
| Acquisitions manager | $2,000 to $3,000 | 5% to 10% |
| Dispo | Varies widely | Percentage of assignment fee |

At $1,500 a month you can hire a genuinely excellent lead manager in Mexico.
That is the band where the candidate quality changes noticeably, so going under
it to save a few hundred dollars is usually a false economy.

## Setting commission

Commission is a retention instrument, not generosity. Someone who leaves every
six months costs more in ramp time, lost pipeline and rehiring than the extra
percentage points ever did.

Pay as much as the model supports. What it supports depends on the spread you
actually run:

- Heavier spreads (flips, wholetail, curative title) support the top of the
  band comfortably.
- Straight wholesaling runs a thinner spread, so the same percentage costs
  proportionally more of the deal. Set expectations accordingly rather than
  promising a number you will resent later.

Whatever you land on, write it down before the offer, including exactly when
commission is earned and when it is paid. Ambiguity here poisons the
relationship at the worst possible moment, which is right after a deal closes.

## Paying people

Pay in the person's local currency through an international payment platform
that handles the conversion. Making an employee absorb conversion cost and
transfer fees out of their own salary is a retention problem you created for
free.

Agree the pay date and hold it exactly. For someone earning a monthly salary in
a different currency, a payment that slips by three days is a real problem in
their life, and it is the fastest way to lose an otherwise happy hire.

## What this does not cover

The bands above are for direct hires. Agencies that place real estate VAs
charge more and handle sourcing and replacement for you. That can be worth it
for a first hire when you have no process yet, but you are paying for the
sourcing rather than for better talent, and this skill exists so you do not
need to.
