# The five roles, what they own, and the one number each is judged on

Every role gets one North Star KPI. Secondary metrics exist, but only one number
decides whether the day was a success, because a person cannot optimize five
things at once and will quietly pick their own if you do not pick for them.

Task lists below are the minimum. Add to them, but do not hire without them:
a person who cannot recite their daily, weekly and monthly tasks will look like
a bad hire when the actual problem is that nobody ever wrote the job down.

---

## Data manager

The first hire for almost every operation. Cheapest role, most documentable, and
the one whose absence breaks everything downstream without making any noise.

**Owns.** List pulls, list hygiene, uploads, skip trace batches, tags and lists
in the CRM, marketing going out on schedule.

**North Star KPI.** Marketing goes out on schedule, every scheduled day, with
the record count the plan called for. Consistency is the metric, not volume.

**Daily.** Pull the day's new records. Push scheduled sequential marketing.
Run skip trace on anything unskipped. Clear the upload error queue.

**Weekly.** Refresh the working lists. Audit tags for drift. Reconcile record
counts against what marketing actually sent. Report the week's numbers.

**Monthly.** Full list health pass: duplicates, dead records, wrong statuses.
Rebuild any preset that has drifted. Archive what is finished.

**Why they fail.** Almost always missing SOPs rather than missing ability. This
is the role where a documented process converts an average hire into a reliable
one, so build the SOPs before they start.

## Prospector

Also called the ninja. Lives on the phone. A specific archetype: most people do
not want to make 200 calls a day, and the ones who do will tell you so in the
first two minutes of the interview.

**Owns.** Outbound dials, voicemails, texts, first contact, qualifying enough to
know whether this is a lead, passing the lead off cleanly.

**North Star KPI.** 150 dials a day minimum, 200 is better, and at least 2 real
leads passed off.

**The open-to-goal alternative.** Set the target at 2 passed-off leads and end
the day when they hit it, whether that takes three hours or twelve. Harder to
game: a dial count can be inflated with hangups and skipped voicemails, a
passed-off lead cannot. Use this when you suspect activity is being manufactured.

**Daily.** Work the assigned queue. Log every attempt. Pass qualified leads to
the lead manager with notes. Hit the number.

**Weekly.** Review call recordings with a manager. Report attempts, contacts,
leads. Flag list quality problems, since a bad list shows up here first.

**Monthly.** Score a sample of their own calls against the rubric. Set one
improvement target for the next month.

**Note.** A full attempt is a call plus a voicemail plus a text, not a single
ring-out. Count attempts that way or the number means nothing.

## Lead manager

Takes the handoff and keeps it warm. This is where most operations leak the most
money, because leads that go cold are invisible on every dashboard.

**Owns.** Every lead after first contact. Qualification depth, follow-up cadence,
next-action discipline, nothing going dark.

**North Star KPI.** Zero leads past their follow-up date. Every active lead has
a scheduled next action.

**Daily.** Work the follow-up queue to zero. Qualify new handoffs on the four
pillars: condition, timeline, motivation, price. Set the next action on every
touch before closing the record.

**Weekly.** Rehash the not-interested and cold pile. Review pipeline by stage.
Report leads by source so marketing spend can be judged.

**Monthly.** Full pipeline audit. Anything with no next action gets one or gets
closed. Report conversion by source.

---

## Acquisitions manager

Makes and closes the offers. Highest-paid role and the one with real commission,
because the spread is directly in their hands.

**Owns.** Offer conversations, price negotiation, contracts signed, seller
relationship through closing.

**North Star KPI.** Contracts signed per month against target.

**Daily.** Run scheduled offer calls. Follow up on outstanding offers. Update
every deal's status and next step.

**Weekly.** Pipeline review with the numbers on each deal. Rework stalled offers.
Report contracts and the reasons behind any losses.

**Monthly.** Close rate by lead source, average spread, average days from lead to
contract.

**Note.** Remote acquisitions works. The belief that this role has to sit in an
office in your market is the most expensive unexamined assumption in the business.

---

## Dispo

Sells the contract. Often the last role hired, and often the one held by the
owner longest.

**Owns.** Buyer list, buyer outreach, assignment, closing coordination.

**North Star KPI.** Days from contract signed to assignment closed.

**Daily.** Push new contracts to the buyer list. Work buyer follow-up. Update
assignment status.

**Weekly.** Add verified buyers to the list. Report days-on-market per contract
and any price movement.

**Monthly.** Prune dead buyers, rank the live ones by what they actually closed,
report average assignment fee.

---

## Setting the number so it holds

State the KPI in the job post, again in the interview, and again on day one.
Three times, same number, before any work starts.

Review it on a fixed cadence the person knows about in advance. A KPI that only
surfaces when someone is already in trouble is a punishment, not a target, and
people manage to the thing they are actually measured on rather than the thing
written on a document they have never reread.
